## Figure summary: environmental matrices as antibiotic reservoirs

This figure compares the major environmental matrices in terms of reservoir breadth, concentration intensity, and prevalent antibiotics. Panel A places each matrix in a breadth-intensity space to distinguish concentrated hotspot reservoirs from diffuse broad reservoirs. Panel B shows the antibiotic-class composition within each matrix, and Panel C shows the most prevalent antibiotics across matrices based on registered measurement frequency.

The analysis indicates that municipal sludge is the main concentrated hotspot reservoir, while sediment is the main broad environmental reservoir with the widest chemical and provincial spread. Soil occupies an intermediate position and is especially enriched for tetracyclines, whereas surface water shows narrower but still meaningful prevalence of sulfonamides and fluoroquinolones.
