## Matrix reservoir analysis

Environmental matrices were compared using the article-quality environmental table after restricting the main reservoir interpretation to the four major non-wastewater matrices: municipal sludge, sediment, soil, and surface water. For each matrix, we quantified direct observation breadth (number of provinces, antibiotic richness, and class richness) and concentration intensity (median, upper-tail, and maximum concentration). These two components were standardized and combined into a reservoir score to distinguish matrices that behaved as broad reservoirs from matrices that behaved mainly as localized concentration hotspots.

### Main findings

- Municipal sludge showed the strongest concentration intensity, with the highest median concentration (42.25) and by far the highest upper-tail burden (90th percentile 2003.70; maximum 4063000.00). This identifies sludge as the main concentrated hotspot reservoir rather than the broadest environmental compartment.
- Sediment showed the broadest reservoir profile, with the highest number of registered measurements (3552), widest province coverage (24 provinces), and greatest chemical richness (121 antibiotics). Although its median concentrations were lower than sludge, sediment retained a substantial upper tail (90th percentile 109.86) and therefore behaved as the main diffuse broad reservoir.
- Soil occupied an intermediate position, with substantial antibiotic richness (69) but lower typical concentration intensity than sludge and lower breadth than sediment. Its profile was especially enriched for tetracycline-family antibiotics.
- Surface water showed moderate typical concentrations (23.09) but far narrower chemical breadth and observation depth than the solid matrices, indicating exposure relevance but weaker support as a long-term reservoir compartment in this dataset.

Across matrices, prevalence was not uniform across antibiotic classes. Municipal sludge was strongly enriched for fluoroquinolones and macrolides at high concentrations, especially ofloxacin, norfloxacin, ciprofloxacin, and azithromycin. Sediment acted as the broadest mixed reservoir, with frequent tetracyclines, sulfonamides, and fluoroquinolones, including oxytetracycline, tetracycline, sulfamethazine, norfloxacin, ciprofloxacin, and ofloxacin. Soil was dominated by tetracyclines and related veterinary-use compounds, especially tetracycline, oxytetracycline, chlortetracycline, doxycycline, and enrofloxacin. Surface water showed a narrower prevalence structure dominated by sulfonamides and fluoroquinolones, notably sulfamethoxazole, norfloxacin, and ciprofloxacin.
